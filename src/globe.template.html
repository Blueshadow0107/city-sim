    People per raster cell (about 37 km across at the equator), log scale. Nearest snapshot: .

    Heat is the Stadestér 1.0 population raster (rebuilt from HYDE 3.x, Reba et al., Chandler, Modelski and others) at quarter resolution: 4×4 blocks of the original 5-arcminute cells are summed. Snapshots are every 1000 years before 0 CE, every 100 to 1700, then every 10; years in between are interpolated in log space, so 2357 BCE sits between the 3000 and 2000 BCE grids. Pale ground is land that has people in at least one snapshot. Frames are decoded on demand, and only the near hemisphere is drawn.

    Dots are the 4,476 cities that ever reach 100,000 (whole urban agglomerations), interpolated between observations. A city is hidden before its first record and held at its last value after the table ends (about 2000). At a given zoom only the largest cities in view are drawn. Dashed lines are the satellite-era (GHSL) urban areas for the four jump-to cities. Time axis is stretched: 2357 BCE–0, 0–1700 and 1700–2025 get 30%, 30% and 40% of the width. Data: Stadestér 1.0 (Zenodo, MIT).
